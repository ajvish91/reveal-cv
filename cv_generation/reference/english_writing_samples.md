# English writing samples (style reference)

Fictional excerpts for **tone and structure** only. Do not treat employers or projects as real biography.

## Connectors and flow

- *since*, *when*, *however*, *at the same time*, *for example*, *that included*, *what draws me to*
- Open with a link between the role and what matters to the candidate; avoid generic enthusiasm.
- Build each point in full sentences rather than punchy fragments.

## Sample 1 (industry platform role)

I am applying for the Senior AI Platform Engineer role on the Developer Platform team in Cloud and Security. Your focus on responsible AI, a paved road for developers, and safe paths from experimentation to production-critical services matches how I like to work: enable teams with solid platforms, clear guardrails, and measurable delivery, not one-off prototypes that never leave a notebook.

Over the past year as a Research Engineer at Northline Labs, I have built and operated LLM pipelines for document processing, with explicit evaluation and strong attention to privacy, cost, and operational reliability. That included moving from expensive external APIs to locally hosted fine-tuned models where control and predictability matter in regulated settings.

## Sample 2 (academic postdoc)

I am applying for the postdoctoral fellowship at the Example AI Decisions Center, hosted by the Department of Computer Science. The centre's focus on safe, trustworthy, and human-centered AI for decision-making in complex professional settings matches the direction I want to develop after doctoral and postdoctoral work on trustworthy automated behaviour, human–AI interaction, and LLM-based decision support.

My background combines experimental AI research with hands-on prototyping for professional users. Recent research engineering work built and evaluated LLM pipelines for document classification, summarization, and content understanding, comparing locally hosted models with external APIs to improve controllability and governance in sensitive workflows.

## Style notes for English application prose

- Clear, calm, explanatory prose. Academic but readable.
- Mix *I*-led sentences with work-led sentences; moderate *I* frequency.
- Four substantive body paragraphs plus a brief closing for letters.
- No bold in letter body except **Re: Role title** in the heading.
- Avoid em-dashes, marketing tone, and colon-led slogan lists.
- Map posting tasks to transferable skills; do not write "I have not used [tool]".

## Academic application letters (researcher / postdoc)

Industry cover letters and academic **application_letter.md** files share the same calm voice, but academic letters should read as a short narrative, not a credential checklist.

- Open on why the **project question** matters to you, then let experience unfold in chronological or thematic order.
- Prefer one developed example per paragraph over "That maps to tasks A, B, and C" lists.
- State fit through **what you would contribute and with whom**, not "my strongest qualification is…" or a closing inventory of skills.
- Acknowledge disciplinary gaps plainly when the posting expects area expertise; show collaboration habits instead of overselling breadth.
- Keep centre-specific project names only when the role genuinely relates; do not treat them as a standalone brag block.
